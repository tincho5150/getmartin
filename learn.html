Buyer Psychology

B2B buyers are not rational actors who evaluate options objectively and choose the best one. They are people — under pressure, managing risk, protecting their careers, navigating internal politics. Understanding the mental models that drive real buying decisions is what separates marketers who create genuine urgency from those who just add more slides to the deck.

75%
of B2B buyers prefer to complete their research without ever talking to a sales rep. By the time they reach out, they've already formed a view. Your job is to shape that view — not overcome it after the fact. The sale happens in the research phase. If you're not present there, you're not in the conversation. — Gartner
60%
Of qualified B2B deals are lost to "no decision" — not to a competitor. Doing nothing is always the default. Make the cost of staying put feel more painful than the cost of moving. — CEB/Gartner
2×
Losses feel twice as powerful as equivalent gains. Reframe your pitch around what the buyer is currently losing — not what they could gain. — Kahneman & Tversky
6.3
Average stakeholders in a B2B purchase. Your champion has to sell your deal to a room you're never in. Give them something that does the selling without you.

92% of B2B buyers are more likely to purchase after reading a trusted review. What others say about you does more commercial work than anything you say about yourself.

Sales Process

Most B2B sales processes fail at predictable moments — the discovery call that turns into a pitch, the demo shown too early, the internal champion who can't close the committee. This track maps how complex B2B deals move — or stall — and where the decisions that determine the outcome are actually made.

The deal is won or lost in Discovery. Everything after is confirming — or undoing — what happened in that first conversation.

01 — Discovery
The most important call you'll turn into a pitch. Discovery is about understanding the buyer's world — what the problem costs, why they haven't solved it, whether you're the right fit. Stop showing the product. Start listening.
02 — Qualify
Not every prospect is a real opportunity. Establish whether budget, authority, need, and timing are real — early. Deals that aren't qualified disappear at proposal stage, after weeks of work.
03
The Demo — by the time they see it, they should already want it.Shown too early, the demo kills deals. It should confirm what the buyer already suspects — not convince someone who isn't ready. A generic feature walkthrough lands for no one.
04 — Proposal
A business case for people you've never met. Written for the committee — not the champion. Restate the problem in their language, quantify the cost of inaction, make the ROI obvious to someone who wasn't in the room.
05 — Close
A confirmation, not a negotiation. The best closes happen when saying yes feels like the obvious, low-risk next step. If you need artificial urgency to close, the earlier stages didn't work.
