Sales Process The stages, the traps, the mechanics

Most B2B sales processes fail at predictable moments — the discovery call that turns into a pitch, the demo shown too early, the internal champion who can't close the committee. This track maps how complex B2B deals move — or stall — and where the decisions that determine the outcome are actually made.

01 — Discovery

The most important call you'll ever turn into a pitch

Discovery is about understanding the buyer's world — what the problem costs them, why they haven't solved it, whether you're the right fit. The moment you start showing the product, you've stopped listening. And the buyer notices.

Questions over features Trap: pitching instead of listening
02 — Qualification

Not every prospect is a real opportunity

Establish early whether budget, authority, need, and timing are real. Deals that aren't qualified early become deals that disappear quietly at proposal stage — after you've spent weeks on them.

Budget · Authority · Need · Timing Trap: hoping it sorts itself out
03 — The Demo

By the time they see the product, they should already want it

Shown too early, the demo kills deals. Shown to the wrong person, it produces no decision. The demo should confirm what the buyer already suspects is true — not try to convince someone who isn't ready.

Tailored · Right people · Right moment Trap: generic feature walkthrough
04 — The Proposal

A business case for people you've never met

A proposal is not a price list. It's a document written for the committee — people who weren't in the room. It should restate the problem in their language, quantify the cost of inaction, and make the ROI obvious without a walkthrough.

Written for the room, not the champion Trap: price without context
05 — The Close

The deal is won or lost in Discovery — not here

If the previous four stages were done well, the close is a confirmation, not a negotiation. The best closes happen when saying yes feels like the obvious, low-risk, well-understood next step — not a leap of faith under deadline pressure.

Confirmation, not persuasion Trap: artificial urgency
